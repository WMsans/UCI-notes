# Reflective Paper on getting into UCI

I’m reflecting on becoming a freshman in UCI. To do that, I would like to bring up some context of me before coming to UCI. I spent most of my time in China, and moved to the US around five years ago. My mother happened to choose Irvine California as the city to live in, because she loves the South California weather and the considerable Asian American population here. 

In contrast of that seemingly casual decision, five years later, when I'm in the place of choosing where I'll live and study for my college education, I have much more solid reasons of staying in Irvine. I had my high school experience here. I did my community services here. I have my friends and connections here, many are also coming to or came from UC Irvine. Thus, in short, I choose Irvine because my home is here. 

One of the things that I'm most excited about is the game development club in the Donald Bren Hall. I've visited their development room a few times when I'm still a high school student, and is very lucky to attend their annual conference of game developers, called Z3. The place is undoubtedly candy land for me, organized by some of the most talented I've seen. Becoming a part of them would be a great honor and learning opportunity for me. I'm also looking forward to participating in undergraduate research and mini internships. 

On the other hand, there are also things that makes me worried. The biggest one is academic rigor. I'm majoring in computer science, and I've heard that the course load here is heavy. The jump from high school to a Research 1 university is a real step up. Time management is also a big part of that worry. Between lectures, problem sets, and projects, I'm afraid I might fall behind if I don't get my schedule under control early. On top of that, wanting to stay involved in the game development club while trying to get into undergraduate research lead to my worries about stretching myself too thin doing all of it at once and ending up doing none of it well.

For this class, I'm hoping to get practical strategies to survive in the college. From the syllabus, I can see the skills it covers, including goal setting, time management, communication, and collaboration, are the ones I would really need to work on. In addition to the lectures, I'm looking forward to learning about campus resources like UROP and the writing center. I also think the Personal Action Plan will be genuinely useful. It's easy to have vague goals like "do well in CS" or "join the game dev club," but I'm hoping this class will help me turn those into a concrete, realistic plan for my first year here.
