# Culture
The culture I identify with most strongly is Chinese culture. One important belief in Chinese culture is that one should maintain their "mianzi", which directly translated to "face" while has nothing to do with physical cheeks. "Mianzi" can be shallowly understood as social reputation, dignity, and prestige, but it has a few more layers of meaning in Chinese language and culture. In Chinese language, "mianzi" can be added, padded, or even borrowed. In other words, it has a volume. The way to build it up is usually through wealth, power, and most importantly, establishing social ties to people. For instance, doing others a favor is essentially building up "mianzi"; on the other hand, one asking for a favor is borrowing their friend's "mianzi". One can give "mianzi" to another person by praising them in public, or lose face by having inappropriate comments. To Chinese people, whenever they go, whoever they socialize with, are all games of maintaining "mianzi". It is so significant that a few of the most important norms derives from it: at a family dinner you would never tell an elder their cooking was bad, one would politely eat it or give a vague compliment to avoid losing face; rather than openly criticizing or rejecting someone, one usually soften their words, hint, or use a third party to deliver negative opinions, and etc. 

A subculture that exists within mainstream culture is the indie game fan/developer community. Its values include artistic integrity, creativity over profit, and collaboration, which you may see in the development process of such games: small studios and fans often share ideas, make free or low-cost games, and treat games as art rather than products. In many ways these values oppose the dominant culture of the gaming industry, where big corporations focus on profit through microtransactions, seasonal passes, and hype-driven marketing. However, despite having differences, it still follows most norms in gaming community, including distributing products on unified platforms, participating in mainstream consumer culture, and relying heavily on player feedback from social media platforms. As a result, they exist harmoniously within it even where their tastes clash. 

In contrast, A counterculture, would be hackers and hacktivists. Their core values that information should be free, that corporate and state control of data is illegitimate, and that anonymity is a right are directly incompatible with dominant culture's values of private property, copyright law, and state authority. Where the mainstream treats unauthorized downloading a paid game as theft, the hacker culture treats copying and sharing as liberation, so the two cannot coexist without one side giving up a central value.

Cultural isomorphism is the process by which different cultures become more and more similar to each other over time. A clear example is McDonaldization: McDonald's restaurants exist in nearly every country, offering the same standardized menu, the same fast-service norms, and the same corporate atmosphere. As people in different societies adopt these shared consumption habits and expectations, local food traditions and eating customs can be weakened or replaced. Over time the cultures converge, becoming less distinct from one another, which is the essence of cultural isomorphism.
