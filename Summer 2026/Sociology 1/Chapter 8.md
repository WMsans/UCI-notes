# Chapter 8: Race and Ethnicity as Lived Experience
## Key Definitions (know these cold)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Race** | A **socially defined category based on real or perceived biological differences** between groups of people — not a biological reality; there are no "pure" races and no race chromosome. |
| **Ethnicity** | A socially defined category based on a **common language, religion, nationality, history, or some other cultural factor** (e.g., the Scotch-Irish; Jews are an ethnic group, not a race). |
| **Symbolic ethnicity** | An ethnic identity that is **relevant only on specific occasions** and does not significantly affect everyday life (St. Patrick's Day, Cinco de Mayo, Nowruz). |
| **Situational ethnicity** | An ethnic identity that **can be either displayed or concealed depending on its usefulness** in a given situation — involves a cost-benefit analysis. |
| **Minority group** | A social group **systematically denied the same access to power and resources** available to society's dominant groups — members are **not necessarily fewer in number** (South Africa under apartheid; California today). |
| **Racism** | An ideology or set of beliefs about the **claimed superiority of one racial or ethnic group over another**; used to justify inequality; often rooted in the assumption that differences are innate/genetic (Kendi 2016). |
| **Prejudice** | A **"prejudgment"** — an inflexible attitude (usually negative, but can be positive) about a group rooted in generalizations or stereotypes ("All Asians are good at math"). |
| **Discrimination** | An **action or behavior that results in unequal treatment** of individuals because of their membership in a certain social group. |
| **Implicit bias** | **Unrecognized or unconscious prejudices and stereotypes** that shape our interactions with others (pediatricians give white patients more pain management than Black patients). |
| **Individual discrimination** | Discrimination carried out by **one person against another** (a racist teacher grading a Hispanic student down). |
| **Institutional discrimination** | Discrimination **carried out systematically by institutions** (government, schools, banks) through policies that affect whole groups (Ferguson PD; FHA redlining). |
| **White nationalism** | The belief that the nation **should be built around a white identity** reflected in religion, politics, economics, and culture. |
| **Privilege** | **Unearned advantages accorded to members of dominant social groups** (male, white, heterosexual, physically able, etc.). |
| **White privilege** | The idea that **whites enjoy unearned advantages not available to nonwhites** and are largely unaware of them (McIntosh's "invisible knapsack"). |
| **Color-blind racism** | (Bonilla-Silva) An ideology that **removes race as an explanation for any form of unequal treatment** — the belief that we live in a society where prejudice and discrimination no longer exist. |
| **Race consciousness** | An ideology that **acknowledges race as a powerful social construct** that shapes our individual and social experiences — the alternative to color blindness. |
| **Microaggressions** | **Everyday uses of subtle verbal and nonverbal communications** that convey denigrating or dismissive messages to members of certain social groups ("What are you?"; clutching a handbag; asking to touch a Black person's hair). |
| **Cultural appropriation** | The **adoption of cultural elements belonging to an oppressed group by members of the dominant group**, without permission and often for the dominant group's gain. |
| **Reverse racism** | The claim that **whites can also suffer discrimination based on race** and experience the same disadvantages as minorities — refuted by data: racism requires institutional power (DiAngelo's "white fragility"). |
| **Antiracist allies** | **Whites and others working toward the goal of ending racial injustice** — often most effective working with other white people. |
| **Critical race theory** | The study of the **relationships among race, racism, and power**; developed in the 1980s by legal scholars; holds that racism permeates social institutions (especially the judicial system); emphasizes intersectionality and narratives from intersecting voices. |
| **Passing** | **Presenting yourself as a member of a different group** than the stigmatized group to which you belong (light-skinned African Americans "living as white"). |
| **Double-consciousness** | (Du Bois) The **divided identity experienced by Black people** in the United States — being Black and American at once. |
| **Code-switching** | **Adjusting one's behavior (especially language) to the norms of the dominant group**; common for Black and Hispanic Americans; comes at a cost — cognitive strain, inauthenticity, burnout. |
| **Miscegenation** | **Romantic, sexual, or marital relationships between people of different races** — once prohibited in 41 of 50 states. |
| **Embodied identity** | Those elements of identity **generated through others' perceptions of our physical traits**; historically the basis for discrimination. |
| **Systemic racism** | Racism **found in the policies and practices of social institutions** (education, economy, military, law enforcement) that results in discrimination against and exclusion of minority groups — neither individual intent nor explicit racist policy is necessary. |
| **Genocide** | The **deliberate and systematic extermination of a racial, ethnic, national, or cultural group**. |
| **Population transfer** | The **forcible removal of a group of people from the territory they have occupied** (Trail of Tears). |
| **Settler colonialism** | The **economic and political subjugation of the minority group by the dominant group within a nation**; often includes physical segregation (Jim Crow South). |
| **Segregation** | The **physical and legal separation of groups by race or ethnicity**. |
| **Assimilation** | A pattern of relations in which the **minority group is absorbed into the dominant group**, making society more homogeneous (the "melting pot"). |
| **Racial assimilation** | The process by which racial minority groups are absorbed into the dominant group **through intermarriage**. |
| **Cultural assimilation** | The process by which racial or ethnic groups are absorbed **by adopting the dominant group's culture**. |
| **Pluralism** | A cultural pattern of intergroup relations that **encourages racial and ethnic variation and acceptance** within a society (the "salad bowl"). |

## 📍 Chapter Opener: Driving While Black

- **Racial profiling** — the practice of suspecting someone has committed a crime **based on their race** — is pervasive on America's roads. Civil rights groups complained about "the stopping of Negroes on foot or in cars without obvious basis" as far back as the 1950s–60s (Harris 1999).
- **Chris Rock** posted three selfies — each taken right after he'd been pulled over by police — putting a real face on the issue.
- **The war on drugs made it worse:** in the 1980s the federal government militarized the border and airports; state and local police searched far more cars. Despite evidence that **drug use rates are similar across racial lines**, nonwhite motorists were disproportionately targeted as "couriers."
- **The legal situation:** traffic codes are "so minutely drawn that virtually every driver will break some rule within a few blocks" — the perfect cover for **pretext stops** (the cited violation is just a pretext to investigate). In **1996 the Supreme Court ruled pretext searches do not violate the Fourth Amendment.**
- **The data (what little exists):**
  - **Missouri (2017 report):** Black drivers **85% more likely** to be pulled over; when stopped, **51% more likely to be searched** — even though white drivers were more often found with contraband.
  - **Oakland, CA:** Black people were 28% of the population but accounted for **60% of police stops**; Black men were **4× more likely** to be searched.
  - **Use of force:** Black men are **2.5× more likely to be killed by police** than white men (Edwards, Lee, and Esposito 2019).
- **Ta-Nehisi Coates,** *Between the World and Me* (2015), to his son: *"The police departments of your country have been endowed with the authority to destroy your body…"*
- **The "talk":** Black parents must teach their children how to interact with police — "Driving while Black is not a crime, but Black parents are forced to tell their children that sometimes it will be treated like it is."
- **"How to Read This Chapter":** race and ethnicity are **socially constructed categories**; when a society categorizes people by race and ethnicity (and all societies do), it creates a **system of stratification that leads to inequality** — wealth, power, privilege, and opportunity are distributed by category. Race and ethnicity also profoundly shape **identity** — bonds to others may be based on shared identities or may transcend them.

> **Lesson:** Race and ethnicity are not biological facts — they are categories whose *meanings* are created, maintained, and modified through social processes in which we all take part.

## 1. Defining Race and Ethnicity

### What Is Race?
- **19th-century biologists** grouped humans into three races — **Negroid, Mongoloid, and Caucasoid** — believed to have separate biological makeups. Modern science rejects this:
  - **No "pure" races** — the lines among races are blurry, not fixed; a person who looks white inevitably has biological material from other races.
  - **Greater genetic diversity *within* racial populations than *between* them** (Koreans differ more from Chinese than from white people).
  - All humans are **99.9% genetically identical**; of the remaining 0.1%, only **15% of variation occurs between geographically distinct groups**. "There's no wiggle room in the human genome for race to be a genetic trait" — **there is no race chromosome.**
  - Blood type differences are biologically more significant than skin color — yet blood types are only used medically, not to sort people into social categories.
- **Physical differences are geographic adaptations:** people near the equator have more **melanin** (protection from sun); people near the poles have less (to absorb enough sunlight to produce **vitamin D**).
- **Sociological definition:** race is a **social category based on real or perceived biological differences** — much more meaningful socially than biologically (Montagu 1998).

### What Is Ethnicity?
- A social category based on **shared ancestry or cultural heritage** — language, religion, history.
- **The Scotch-Irish** — a distinct American ethnic group (Vance's *Hillbilly Elegy*, 2016): tight-knit, fiercely loyal, evangelical Christianity, conservative politics.
- **Jewish people are an ethnic group, not a race** (contra the Nazis and white supremacists) — a blond, blue-eyed Jew from Scandinavia and a Black Ethiopian Jew both share the culture.

### The Social Construction of Race: Who Counts as "White"?
- **Early 1900s:** native-born white Protestants did **not** consider Irish, Italian, or Jewish immigrants white — restricting where they could live and work and forcing them into urban "ghettos" (Brodkin 1999; Ignatiev 1996, 2008).
- **After WWII:** as the second generation grew up, ethnic identity declined and **skin color became the main white/nonwhite line**.
- **Today:** are people of Middle Eastern descent white? In the post-9/11 climate, **Arabs and Muslims have been symbolically labeled "nonwhite"** in significant and harmful ways.

### "Ethnic Options": Symbolic and Situational Ethnicity
- **Symbolic ethnicity:** displayed only on special occasions — St. Patrick's Day (parades, green beer, Chicago's **green river**, corned beef and cabbage), Passover, Cinco de Mayo, Nowruz.
- **Situational ethnicity:** asserted in some situations, downplayed in others — a **cost-benefit analysis**. **Dr. Ferris's Lebanese ancestry:** downplayed in Southern California (political climate of suspicion toward Arabs), but a valuable asset when she moved to **Peoria, Illinois** (a large Lebanese-descent population — the mayor, councilman, state senator, congressman, and prominent families were all Lebanese).
- **The catch (Mary Waters 1990):** *"The social and political consequences of being Asian or Hispanic or black are not, for the most part, symbolic, nor are they voluntary. They are real, unavoidable, and sometimes hurtful."* **White people have "ethnic options"; nonwhite people do not** — visible group membership is not a choice.

> **📋 Data Workshop — Displaying Ethnicity:** participant observation at a St. Patrick's Day parade, ethnic festival (St. Anthony's Feast Day, LA's African Marketplace), or ethnic neighborhood (Philadelphia's Italian Market, Chicago's Ukrainian Village/Greektown/Pilsen, Solvang CA). Even tiny **Postville, Iowa (pop. 2,200)** has Hasidic Jewish, Mexican, Guatemalan, Ukrainian, Nigerian, Bosnian, and Czech residents. Field notes on dress, music, food, language, traditions (buying a goldfish for spring, wrapping tamales, the claddagh ring). *Prep-Pair-Share* or a 3-page paper.

## 2. The U.S. Population by Race

- **2019 (U.S. Census):** **60% non-Hispanic white, 19% Hispanic, 13% Black, 6% Asian, ~1% American Indian.** *Hispanic origin counts as an ethnicity, not a race — Hispanics may be of any race, so the total exceeds 100%.*
- **2000 Census:** Americans could identify with **more than one race for the first time** — creating **57 possible racial combinations**. Multiracial people have existed since the European settlers mixed with American Indians and enslaved Black people alike.
- **Minority ≠ fewer in number.** Sociological definition: a group **denied equal access to power and resources**. *South Africa:* Black people outnumbered whites **7:1**, yet a small white minority controlled the country until Mandela's 1994 election.
- **Majority-minority states:** California (2019: 37% white — nonwhite groups together >60%), plus Hawaii, Texas, New Mexico, DC. **Whites remain the dominant group** in power, resources, and representation even where they're not the numeric majority — Hispanics/Latinos are underrepresented in the UC system and state government but **overrepresented in prisons, poverty counts, and violent-crime victimization**.
- **The demographic shift:** whites were **85% of the U.S. population in 1965** → **60% today** → projected **49.7% by 2045**, making the U.S. a **majority-minority country** (Frey 2018b).
- **Master status:** minority membership can override other statuses (gender, age) and generates common identity and solidarity among members.

## 3. Racism in Its Many Forms

- Racism provides the ideological **support for unequal social arrangements** — rooted in beliefs that differences are innate/genetic or in negative views of a group's culture.
- **Covid-19 and anti-Asian racism:** since the virus was first identified in Wuhan, Asian Americans became scapegoats — **hate crimes against Asian Americans rose ~150% in 2020** (and are underreported — many victims fear the police).
- **George Floyd (May 2020) and shifting attitudes:** **57% of Americans** now believe police are more prone to use excessive force on Black Americans (up from **33% in 2014**); **>3/4 of Americans** say discrimination against racial/ethnic minorities is a "big problem" (vs. half in 2015); Americans are more pessimistic about racial progress than in 2014.

### Prejudice and Discrimination
- **Prejudice** (a "prejudgment") is an inflexible *attitude* rooted in stereotypes — it can even be "positive" ("All Asians are good at math") and can flow from minorities toward the dominant group, or toward one's own group (**internalized racism**, Pyke 2010). We may unknowingly absorb prejudice through socialization.
- **Discrimination** is *behavior* — unequal treatment based on group membership.
- **Merton's "timid bigots" (1949):** prejudiced but don't act on it (a teacher who believes the stereotype but doesn't grade accordingly).
- **Discrimination without prejudice:** a non-prejudiced person carries out institutional policies with discriminatory outcomes (promoting by seniority privileges dominant-group members who have longer tenure).
- **Implicit bias:** unrecognized/unconscious prejudice — pediatricians give **white patients more pain management** (Sabin and Greenwald 2012); students of color are **disciplined more often and more harshly** (Lhamon and Samuels 2014).
- **Individual vs. institutional discrimination:** one person acting unfairly vs. institutions (government, schools, banks) whose policies affect whole groups.
- **Ferguson, Missouri (2014):** after police killed unarmed **Michael Brown** and a grand jury declined to indict, a DOJ investigation found the police department routinely violated Black residents' constitutional rights. In a city that was **67% Black**: Black residents were **85% of vehicle stops, 88% of use-of-force cases, and 93% of arrests**.
- **Rothstein, *The Color of Law* (2017):** the **Federal Housing Authority in the 1930s explicitly refused to insure mortgages for Black borrowers** — in Black *and* white neighborhoods. This policy, **redlining** (Black neighborhoods designated on federal maps), severely restricted Black homeownership; now illegal, **but the segregation it produced continues**.

### White Nationalism
- **KKK, Aryan Brotherhood, neo-Nazis** believe whites are innately superior and should control all institutions — they want an all-white nation. These ideas overlap with the populist **alt-right**.
- **Visible in recent years:** **Charlottesville "Unite the Right" rally (Aug 2017)** erupted in violence; **white nationalist symbols appeared in the Capitol riots (Jan 2021)**; Trump's anti-immigrant framing dovetailed with the movement.
- **Joe Feagin:** white supremacy is **nothing new** — it dates to the country's founding; "racism today remains extensive, foundational, and systemic." These views are held by **many ordinary whites**, not just a fringe (Picca and Feagin 2007).
- **Arlie Hochschild, *Strangers in Their Own Land* (2018):** rural Louisiana Tea Party supporters see themselves as **"waiting in line"** for the benefits of American life and view immigrants and other nonwhite groups as **"line-cutters."**
- **SPLC (2020):** total hate groups *declined* from a record **1,020 in 2018**, but **white nationalist groups surged 55% since 2017** and anti-immigrant groups also grew.

### White Privilege and Color-Blind Racism
- Roots: **W.E.B. Du Bois (1903)**; reintroduced by **Peggy McIntosh (1988)** — "unpacking the invisible knapsack" of white privilege.
- **White privilege = unearned advantages whites mostly don't notice:** assuming authority figures will be white (reduced fear of unjust treatment); finding makeup, underclothes, and "nude" products that match your skin; **pointe shoes** — nonwhite ballerinas had to dye "white" shoes until **brown pointe shoes became commercially available in 2018**.
- **Color-blind racism (Bonilla-Silva 2003/2017, 2020):** the claim "I don't see race" sounds good but is a *new, more "civilized" form of racism* — it denies that race matters and attributes any remaining differences to nonracial dynamics. ("We elected a Black president" ≠ postracial world.)
- **The alternative: race consciousness** — acknowledging race as a powerful factor in everyday life and institutions; recognizing one's own privilege as part of the structure of inequality.

### Microaggressions
- **Racial microaggressions (Sue 2010):** small-scale racial slights, insults, and misperceptions in everyday interactions — subtle, casual, often unintentional, but they denigrate and marginalize.
- Examples: "**What are you?**" / "**Where are you really from?**"; a white woman **clutching her handbag** when passing Latino men; asking to **touch a Black person's hair**; being mistaken for a service worker; taxis passing by people of color; "compliments" that reaffirm stereotypes ("You're so smart" to Asian people; "You look exotic" to multiracial people).
- **Consequences:** not just discomfort — elevated risk of **depression and suicide** (O'Keefe et al. 2015; Torino 2017). Jordan Peele's horror film ***Get Out*** dramatizes everyday microaggressions.

### Cultural Appropriation
- The dominant group **adopts, co-opts, or takes cultural elements** (art, music, dance, dress, language, rituals) from a marginalized group **for its own advantage** — Halloween costumes, college theme parties, fashion runways (suede, fringe, moccasins, turquoise).
- **Native mascots:** it took until **2021** for Cleveland's MLB team to drop the "Indians" name and mascot. Sacred items (headdresses, sweat lodges) used disrespectfully; the dominant group **turns an oppressed group's symbols into commodities for profit** — "postmodern cultural imperialism."
- **Hollywood whitewashing:** Matt Damon (Chinese warlord, *The Great Wall*), Scarlett Johansson (Japanese anime character, *Ghost in the Shell*), Emma Stone (multiracial Asian, *Aloha*) — widely criticized.

### Reverse Racism
- The claim that **whites suffer race-based discrimination like minorities do**. A poll: **36% of Americans** believe whites experience "a lot of discrimination" — but only **20% of Black respondents** agreed vs. **40% of white respondents** (Edwards-Levy 2019).
- **Why it's a myth:** whites may face situational or occasional slights, but they do not face widespread cumulative disadvantage in every sphere; even a society full of prejudiced people of color couldn't deny whites education, jobs, homes, or legal protection. **Racism requires institutional power** (Robin DiAngelo 2012, who coined **"white fragility"**).

### Affirmative Action
- Policies/programs/practices to **create opportunities for underrepresented minorities** in housing, education, and employment; goals: diversity, inclusion, equal access, reducing the effects of historical discrimination.
- **Legal battles:** white students suing universities (claims of "reverse racism") have reached the Supreme Court and been struck down; a suit against **Harvard on behalf of Asian American applicants** claims its race-conscious admissions are discriminatory. Several states (e.g., **California**) have **banned affirmative action** in higher education.

### Antiracist Allies
- People of color have necessarily led the struggle; whites can work **in solidarity** — most effectively by **working with other white people** to raise awareness and confront racism in everyday life.
- **Kendi's *How to Be an Antiracist* (2019);** organizations: **Showing Up for Racial Justice (SURJ)**, the Unitarian Universalist Association (UUA); white peer-group meetings in schools, churches, workplaces.

### 📡 In the Future: Whose Lives Matter?
- **Black Lives Matter** arose in response to the extrajudicial killings of Black men, **starting with Trayvon Martin**; co-founded by **Opal Tometi, Alicia Garza, and Patrisse Cullors**. Its mission: make the U.S. safer for Black people by acknowledging **systemic racism**.
- **Systemic racism needs no individual intent or explicitly racist policy** — it's built into structures *because it was part of our founding principles* (the Constitution legally dehumanized people of African descent).
- **BLM's claim on law enforcement:** officers shoot to kill where little threat exists, are less likely to be charged/convicted when they shoot Black people; cameras (cell phones, dashcams, bodycams) made it visible. The temptation: blame the victim or "bad apples" — BLM asks us to **look at the system**.
- **What can you do?** Learn real history (**Howard Zinn, *A People's History of the United States* (1995); Ibram X. Kendi, *Stamped from the Beginning* (2016)**); listen to people whose experiences differ; act — march, refuse to laugh at racist jokes, patronize businesses owned by people of color, write letters, have hard conversations.

## 4. Theoretical Approaches to Understanding Race

### Structural Functionalism
- Best explains how **European immigrant groups (Irish, Italians)** assimilated into the larger society; **less successful** at explaining why Black and Hispanic Americans maintain distinct identities — because functionalism treats any persistent phenomenon (including racial inequality) as contributing to social stability.
- On prejudice: groups are **ethnocentric** (one's own culture is "right and normal"); positive feelings bind groups together, but cohesion breeds unfavorable views of outsiders; cultural differences and lack of integration feed fear and hostility.

### Conflict Theory
- **Edna Bonacich (1980):** racism is partly driven by **economic competition** — the **"split labor market"** (one group of workers routinely paid less) keeps minority wages low, compounding racism with poverty.
- **William Julius Wilson (1980):** openly racist policies created a **Black underclass**, but it is now perpetuated by **economic factors**, not racial ones.
- **Tomás Almaguer, *Racial Fault Lines* (2008):** 19th-century California racial hierarchy — **whites at the top, then Mexicans, Black Americans, Asians, and American Indians at the bottom**; "manifest destiny" justified land-taking; "uncivilized heathens" justified killing.
- **Omi and Winant (1994, 2015):** race is not secondary to class — it **permeates lived experience, the economy, and government**.
- **Intersectionality:** **Patricia Hill Collins (2006), bell hooks (1990), Gloria Anzaldúa (1987)** — race must be explained as it is *experienced*, intersecting with class and gender; race is created symbolically in everyday interactions (sharing common ground with interactionism).
- **Critical race theory (Bell 1980; Delgado and Stefancic 2012; Williams 1991):** developed in the **1980s by legal scholars**; racism permeates institutions (especially the **judicial system**); emphasizes **intersectionality** and narratives from many voices; committed to challenging racist laws and promoting social justice — some practitioners engage in **public sociology** (dialogue beyond academia).

### Symbolic Interactionism
- Race is **created and interpreted in everyday life** — meanings change over time.
- **Omi and Winant:** which features are chosen to make racial distinctions "is always and necessarily a **social and historical process**"; the definitions are "at best imprecise and at worst completely arbitrary" — yet race is a "fundamental dimension of social organization and cultural meaning in the U.S."
- **Stuart Hall's son:** told "You're Black," the two-and-a-half-year-old replied, "**No. I'm brown**" — thinking in physical terms. If race were biological, the boy would be right; but race is a *social* category framed in terms of biological features.
- **Table 8.1 — Theory in Everyday Life (Racial Inequality):**
  - **Structural functionalism:** racial inequality has functions that maintain social order — e.g., creating cohesion within both dominant and minority groups.
  - **Conflict theory:** differences create intergroup conflict — e.g., white people (and men) object to affirmative action programs, creating conflict among racial groups.
  - **Symbolic interactionism:** race/ethnicity is part of identity displayed through presentation of self — some (white ethnics, light-skinned nonwhites) can conceal it, escaping inequality *as individuals* without erasing it from society.

### Passing, Code-Switching, and Embodied Identities
- **Racial passing** — living as a member of a different racial category — has a long history: after slavery, light-skinned African Americans "lived as white" to avoid the consequences of being Black. Ties into Du Bois's **double-consciousness**: can one be Black *and* claim one's rights as an American?
- **Code-switching:** adjusting behavior (especially language) to white norms. **Nearly half of Black college graduates** say they feel the need to change how they talk around other races (Pew). It helps hiring/promotion (lessens association with negative stereotypes) **but causes cognitive strain, inauthenticity, and burnout** (McCluney et al. 2019).
- **Embodied vs. disembodied identity:** online, physical cues disappear — touted as democratizing. **But anonymity "unmasks" covert racism:** racist rhetoric is common on Twitter, and exposure to "unmasked" racism on an anonymous forum disrupted students of color's relationship to their university (Eschmann 2020).

### 💞 In Relationships: From the Lovings to Megxit
- **Miscegenation** was once prohibited in **41 of 50 states**. In **1958**, **Mildred (Black) and Richard Loving (white)** married in Virginia, were arrested under the state's "Act to Preserve Racial Purity," and were banished (sentence suspended on condition they leave). In **1967 the Supreme Court (Loving v. Virginia) overturned all such laws**. **Alabama didn't overturn its last antimiscegenation statute until 2000.**
- Legal ≠ easy: interracial couples still face stigma, in-group pressure, accusations of disloyalty, and stereotypes.
- **The numbers:** interracial marriages rose from **0.4% (1960) → 10% (2015) → 17% among newlyweds** (Livingston and Brown 2017). Most common among **Asians (29% of newlyweds)** and **Hispanics (27%)**, then **Black (18%)** and **white (11%)**. Gender skew: **Asian women** marry out far more than Asian men; **Black women** far less than Black men. College-educated people intermarry more.
- **Attitudes:** in 2017, **39% of Americans** said marrying someone of a different race is good for society. Non-Black adults opposed to a close relative marrying a Black person: **63% in 1990 → 14% in 2017**. Disapproval for Hispanic/Asian partners: 9%; white: 4%.
- **"Megxit":** Prince Harry and Meghan Markle's interracial marriage was historic; the couple spoke candidly about racism and stepped away from royal life.

## 5. Race, Ethnicity, and Life Chances

- **Opening vignette — Patricia Williams (1997):** a Black law professor with an excellent credit history gets a mortgage over the phone, discovers the representative checked "white," corrects it to "African American" — and suddenly the bank demands a bigger down payment and higher interest rate (motivated by "falling property values" — caused by her). **Membership in a racial category carries a high price.**

### 👨‍👩‍👧 Family
- **Marriage:** 52% of white households and 47% of Hispanic households include a married couple — but only **28% of Black households** (Census 2020a).
- **Children living with two parents (2020):** **79% of white children, 68% of Hispanic children, 41% of Black children** — nearly half of Black children live with their mother only.
- **Kathryn Edin and Maria Kefalas (2005):** low-income women of all ethnicities see marriage as offering **few benefits** — the men they're likely to encounter won't offer financial stability, respectability, or trust worth the risk (love isn't the issue).
- **Teen birth rates (2019, per 1,000):** white **11**, Black **26**, Hispanic **25**. **Angela Y. Davis (2001):** Black teenage girls see fewer educational/work opportunities and choose motherhood; punishing teen mothers won't work — only attacking racism in education and the workforce will.

### 🩺 Health
- **Life expectancy (2018):** white males **76**, white females **81**; Black males **71**, Black females **78**; **Hispanics live longest — 79 (men) and 84 (women)**, higher even than whites.
- **Maternal and infant mortality:** Black mothers die from pregnancy complications at a rate **>3×** that of other mothers; **infant mortality for Black babies is >2×** that of white babies.
- **"Weathering" (Geronimus et al. 2006):** exposure to racism erodes health — racially differentiated stressors → more chronic disease, lower life expectancy. Minorities also face more workplace dangers, environmental toxins, and violence.
- **Covid-19 was NOT the "great equalizer":** Black Americans **2×** more likely to die than whites; Hispanics **2.3×**; American Indians **2.4×**. Why: essential/frontline work, crowded housing, unequal access to care, and discrimination in the health-care system.
- **Uninsured (2018, nonelderly):** **19% of Hispanics and 12% of Black people** vs. 8% of whites and 7% of Asians (ACA reduced rates but disparities remain).

### 🎓 Education
- **High school graduation (2019):** Asian **93%**, white **89%**, Hispanic **82%**, Black **80%**. Hispanic dropout rate is highest — **8%, nearly 2× that of white students**.
- **Victor Rios (2017) — school-to-prison pipeline:** Latino youth misread by teachers and school authorities get labeled "bad," "deviant," "at risk," face greater scrutiny and punishment (suspension, expulsion) — criminalizing them, while police surveil them in their communities.
- **Bachelor's degrees (2020, 25+):** Asian **61%**, white **41%**, Black **28%**, Hispanic **21%**.
- **Stereotype threat (Claude Steele 2010):** in demanding, high-stakes situations (tests), the fear of confirming negative stereotypes hampers Black students' performance; in unstressful situations they perform as well as peers. **Stereotype promise (Lee and Zhou 2015):** the positive "model minority" stereotype gives Asian American students a boost in the same situations (see [[Chapter 6]]).

### 💼 Work and Income
- **Occupational segregation:** in 2020, **37% of home health aides and 38% of postal clerks were Black**; **Hispanics were 46% of construction workers and 46% of maids/housecleaners** — mostly semiskilled/unskilled jobs. Low-wage jobs have **shifted from Black to Hispanic workers** over time (private household cleaners: Black 42% in 1983 → 17% in 2020) — people of color increasingly compete with *each other* for these jobs.
- **Median household income (2019):** Asian **$98,174**, white **$76,057**, Hispanic **$56,113**, Black **$45,438** — Black households earn **60%** and Hispanic **74%** of white households' median income.
- **Poverty (2019):** **9% of whites, 19% of African Americans, 16% of Hispanics.**
- **Bertrand and Mullainathan (2004) — résumé experiment:** identical résumés with **white-sounding names (Emily, Greg) got 50% more callbacks** than Black-sounding names (Lakisha, Jamal); adding qualifications helped white applicants **30% more** than Black applicants. Discrimination occurred regardless of occupation or industry.

### 🏢 On the Job: Diversity Programs — Do They Work?
- **Why they're needed:** at the end of 2020 there were only **3 African American CEOs and 41 female CEOs** of Fortune 500 companies (<1% and 7%).
- **Dobbin and Kalev (2016)** — three decades of data from **800+ firms** + hundreds of interviews:
  - **Top-down approaches don't work:** mandatory diversity training (effects fade after a few days — sometimes adverse, evoking anger/resistance), hiring tests, performance ratings.
  - **Voluntary training works better:** +**9–13%** women and Black/Hispanic/Asian men in management after five years.
  - **Most effective:** engaging managers in solving the problem, increasing contact with minority workers, and creating **social accountability** — **mentoring, college recruitment programs** (+10% women in management after five years), **diversity task forces and diversity managers** (when hiring decisions are reviewed, managers consider all qualified applicants).
- **Backlash:** experiments show **pro-diversity messages make white male applicants feel threatened** — they express more discrimination concerns and perform worse (Dover, Major, and Kaiser 2016).
- After George Floyd (2020), organizations added **antiracist training** — controversial (Trump briefly banned it in federal agencies as "divisive"). Starbucks closed all U.S. stores for a day of diversity training after two Black men were removed for sitting in the store.

### ⚖️ Criminal Justice
- **Prison population (2018):** **33% Black, 30% white, 23% Hispanic** — even though whites are ~60% of the population. **Black men are imprisoned at a rate nearly 6× that of white men.**
- **Policing data (ACLU 2008):** Black and Hispanic Americans are **127% more likely to be stopped** and **43% more likely to be frisked** — yet **42% (Black) and 32% (Hispanic) less likely** to be in possession of a weapon or drugs.
- **Crack vs. powder cocaine:** federal law until 2010 handed far tougher sentences for **crack** (disproportionately Black users) than **powder** (disproportionately white/Hispanic users) — 5 grams of crack = the same mandatory **10-year sentence** as 500 grams of powder. In 2010, **79% of federally sentenced crack offenders were Black**, though two-thirds of crack users were white or Hispanic. The **Fair Sentencing Act of 2010** reduced the disparity from **100:1 to 18:1** — but that's still 18× harsher, not retroactive, and federal-only (state penalties remain far stiffer).
- **Homicide victims (2018):** **54% of murder victims were Black**, though Black people are 13% of the population.
- **Hate crimes (2019):** over half were race/ethnicity/ancestry-based — **48% targeting Black Americans**, 16% white, 14% Hispanic, 4% Asian.

### 🔀 Intersectionality
- **France Winddance Twine (2011b):** white women who form families with Black men effectively **lose some of their whiteness** — becoming **"honorary Blacks"** in the eyes of their white working-class friends and family — a punishment not meted out to white men who marry Black women. **Interracial intimacy and its racism is a gendered experience.**
- **Motherhood as an intersecting status:** white mothers of interracial children often lack the **"racial literacy"** to raise children who will be seen and treated as Black (hair care, cooking, preparing for racism) — race, class, gender, and parenthood combine to shape lived experience.

## 6. Intergroup Relations: Conflict or Cooperation

Five patterns of intergroup relations, **from the most violent to the most tolerant**. Some groups (e.g., American Indians) suffer several patterns over time.

### 1. Genocide (worst outcome)
- Deliberate, systematic extermination of a racial, ethnic, national, or cultural group — past *and* present.
- **Armenians:** ~**1.5 million** massacred by the Turkish government, 1915–1923 — the "forgotten genocide" (Hitler is alleged to have asked, "Who remembers the Armenians?").
- **Nazi Germany** killed **two-thirds of the Jews of Europe**.
- Modern cases: **Darfur**, **Milošević's ethnic cleansing in the Balkans**, the **Hutu slaughter of Tutsi in Rwanda**.
- **Native Americans:** systematically killed by European colonists (beyond disease); total killed estimated at **15 million up to 100 million**.

### 2. Population Transfer
- The **forcible removal** of a group from its territory.
- **Trail of Tears (1838–39):** Georgia and the federal government marched **17,000 Cherokees** 800+ miles west of the Mississippi; **4,000+ died** of hunger, exposure, or disease. Reservations remain the template.
- **Today:** the **Israeli government restricts Palestinian movement** in the West Bank and Gaza, building barriers to wall them in and keep them separate.
- **Indirect form:** make life so miserable people leave "voluntarily" — the **Mormons** (1846–69), 70,000 of whom crossed the country to Utah after religious persecution.

### 3. Settler Colonialism and Segregation
- **Colonialism:** a stronger nation takes control of a weaker one to extend territory or exploit resources (British Empire: India, Burma, the West Indies, South Africa, Australia).
- **Settler colonialism:** exploitation of a minority group **within the dominant group's own borders** — economic exploitation + physical segregation.
- **Jim Crow South (up to the 1960s):** separate neighborhoods, "coloreds"-only sections of buses, parks, restaurants; segregation also pervaded the **U.S. military and professional sports**. **Jackie Robinson** broke baseball's color barrier in **1947**.

### 4. Assimilation
- The minority group is **absorbed into the dominant group** — the "**melting pot**." If everyone belongs to the same group, conflict decreases.
- **Twentieth-century immigrants** (Irish, Italians, Eastern Europeans) were once "ethnics" — now practically unrecognizable as distinct groups; they assimilated into the category of "white Americans."
- **Two forms:** **racial assimilation** (absorbed through intermarriage until races are mixed) and **cultural assimilation** (adopting the dominant culture) — often both at once.
- **Not always voluntary:** groups may be *forced* to give up their religion and language — many American Indians lost tribal languages and cultural practices within a few generations.

### 5. Pluralism (most tolerant)
- **Encourages** racial and ethnic variation — the "**salad bowl**," in which ingredients keep their distinct qualities; also called **multiculturalism**. Core value: tolerance.
- **Canada as the model:** two official linguistic groups (English and French) plus European, Chinese, Indian immigrants and First Nations peoples — with government funding for multicultural programs.
- **U.S. backlash:** critics attack ethnic studies, women's studies, LGBTQ studies in the curriculum, and bilingual/ESL education — despite research showing benefits (Krashen 1996). As the racial/ethnic mix grows, Americans may yet incorporate multiculturalism into national identity.

### 🌍 Global Perspective: The Humanitarian Crisis at the Border
- **Who's coming:** migration from Mexico (especially illegal) has dropped; most border migrants now come from Central America's **Northern Triangle — El Salvador, Guatemala, and Honduras**. The number of Northern Triangle-born people living in the U.S. rose **78% between 2010 and 2019**.
- **2019:** over **850,000 people** were apprehended at the southern border — **~80% from the Northern Triangle**, nearly **two-thirds families or unaccompanied minors**.
- **Push factors:** economic instability, political turmoil, corruption, crime, gang violence and recruitment, domestic violence, drug trafficking, climate change — and in Nov 2020, back-to-back hurricanes displaced hundreds of thousands. Most people in Guatemala and Honduras live below the poverty line. Many seek asylum as "their best chance of survival."
- **The Trump administration's response:** **family separation policy** (April–June 2018) — "zero tolerance": parents prosecuted and jailed rather than held with children; **~2,000 children separated**, held in shelters and foster care; "kids in cages" media reports sparked public outcry and suspension of the policy. During Covid, migrants were turned back under an emergency public health law; **~400,000 apprehended in 2020**.
- **Under Biden:** for the first months of 2021 the same public health law continued turning people away; **June 2021: ~190,000 apprehensions — a twenty-year high**. Exceptions for minors, domestic-violence survivors, and transgender persons — but the vast majority were denied entry.

## Closing Comments

- Constructing racial/ethnic categories **seems inevitably to lead to stratification, inequality, and destructive processes** (stereotyping, segregation, prejudice, discrimination).
- **But there are positive consequences:** racial and ethnic categories create **identity, unity, and solidarity** — a sense of belonging to something larger, of cultural connection and shared history (ethnic festivals and holidays).
- **The key sociological insight:** because race and ethnicity are **socially constructed**, their *meanings* are socially constructed too. Historically we have built meanings that favor some and exploit others — **is it possible to construct meanings that value and celebrate all groups?** The authors hope you'll be part of that transformation.

## Quick Memory Hooks
- **Race = social category, not biology:** 99.9% of humans are genetically identical; more genetic diversity *within* races than *between* them; no race chromosome; skin color = melanin/vitamin D adaptation. Ethnicity = shared culture (language, religion, history) — Jews are an ethnic group, not a race.
- **"White" is a moving boundary:** Irish/Italian/Jewish immigrants weren't white in the early 1900s → became white after WWII → today Middle Easterners are being symbolically labeled nonwhite.
- **Ethnic options are a white privilege:** symbolic ethnicity (St. Patrick's Day) and situational ethnicity (Ferris's Lebanese ancestry: downplay in CA, asset in Peoria) — nonwhite people can't opt in and out (Waters).
- **Minority ≠ numerical minority:** South Africa (7:1 Black to white, whites ruled until 1994); California is majority-minority but whites still dominate power. Whites: 85% (1965) → 60% → projected 49.7% (2045) — majority-minority country.
- **Racism needs institutional power:** prejudice (attitude) vs. discrimination (action); "timid bigots" (Merton); implicit bias (pain management, school discipline); individual vs. institutional discrimination (Ferguson: 67% Black city — 85% of stops, 88% of force, 93% of arrests; FHA redlining).
- **Racism's modern forms:** color-blind racism (Bonilla-Silva) → race consciousness; microaggressions (Sue) — "What are you?"; cultural appropriation (mascots, whitewashing); reverse racism is a myth (36% believe it; 40% of whites vs 20% of Blacks); DiAngelo's white fragility.
- **White nationalism isn't fringe:** Charlottesville 2017, Capitol riots 2021, +55% white nationalist groups since 2017 (SPLC); Hochschild's "line-cutters."
- **Theories:** Functionalism = assimilation + ethnocentrism (weak on persistent inequality); Conflict = split labor market (Bonacich), Black underclass (Wilson), racial formation (Omi & Winant), **critical race theory** (racism in institutions, intersectionality, narratives); Interactionism = race is made in everyday interaction (Hall's "I'm brown"), **passing**, **code-switching** (half of Black college grads change how they talk), embodied vs. disembodied identities (the internet unmasks racism).
- **Interracial marriage:** 0.4% (1960) → 10% (2015) → 17% of newlyweds; Loving v. Virginia 1967; Alabama held out until 2000; disapproval of a relative marrying Black: 63% (1990) → 14% (2017); Megxit.
- **Life chances by race (2019–2020):** family (28% of Black households are married couples; 41% of Black kids live with two parents; teen births 26/25 vs 11), health (Black men live to 71 vs white 76; Hispanics live longest — 84 for women; "weathering"; Covid deaths 2–2.4×), education (HS grad 80% Black vs 93% Asian; BA+ 61% Asian vs 21% Hispanic; stereotype threat vs. promise; Rios's school-to-prison pipeline), work (Black income = 60% of white; 50% callback gap in résumé study; Black → Hispanic shift in low-wage jobs), justice (Black men imprisoned 6×; 127% more stops; crack 100:1 → 18:1; 54% of murder victims Black; hate crimes 48% target Black Americans).
- **Diversity programs:** mandatory training backfires; voluntary training +9–13% management gains; mentoring, college recruitment, and diversity task forces (accountability) work best; pro-diversity messages threaten white men (Dover et al.).
- **Five patterns of intergroup relations (most violent → most tolerant):** genocide (Armenians 1.5M; Holocaust; Rwanda; Native Americans) → population transfer (Trail of Tears: 17,000 Cherokees, 4,000+ dead; Israel/Palestine; Mormons) → settler colonialism & segregation (Jim Crow; Jackie Robinson 1947) → assimilation (melting pot; Irish/Italians became "white"; forced loss of language) → pluralism (salad bowl; Canada; backlash against ethnic studies).
- **The border crisis:** Northern Triangle (El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras) +78% since 2010; 850,000+ apprehensions 2019; family separation (~2,000 kids, Apr–Jun 2018); June 2021: 190,000 — 20-year high.
- **BLM:** founded after Trayvon Martin by Tometi, Garza, Cullors; systemic racism needs no intent; learn (Zinn, Kendi), listen, act.
- **Bottom line:** race and ethnicity are socially constructed — so their *meanings* can be reconstructed. Categories create identity and solidarity as well as stratification. Who decides what race means, and who benefits from the answer — that's the sociological question.
