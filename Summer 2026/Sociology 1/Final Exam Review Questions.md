## Groups

1. What are the features of groups?
	1. Recognition
	2. Interaction
	3. Shared norm
	4. Shared goal
2. What are social networks and how can we use them to study groups?
	1. Social networks are the connection between people built by social ties. 
	2. We study groups using the network: 
		1. Network shapes the group's behavior
		2. Idea spread with groups 
3. What leads to feelings of alienation? Describe Durkheim and Putnam's arguments.
	1. *Alienation is anomie*. 
	2. Durkheim: 
		1. Feelings of alienation in rapid social change
	3. Putnam: 
		1. Bowling Alone
		2. We are alienated because we choose to quit activities offline. 
4. Describe a highly cohesive group.
	1. A highly cohesive group is where members of the group are highly alike, share a strong common norms or goals, or are tightly connected together. 
	2. *social cohesion* is where members want to get together
	3. *task cohesion* is where members want to achieve the same goal. 
5. How does the illusion of out-group homogeneity increase in-group cohesion?
	1. When facing a strong out-group, the in-group members would form a sense of competition (*ethnocentrism*, or the desire to be superior), and thus increase in-group cohesion. 
	2. us vs. them intensifies loyalty and cohesion within the group
6. What impact does intergroup conflict have on processes internal to the group (do groups benefit in certain ways from intergroup conflict)?
	1. Ethnocentrism
	2. More cohesive
	3. *Leaders becomes more militant*
	4. *Winning the conflict becomes the priority*
7. Describe reference groups/social comparison theory.
	1. Reference groups are *membership to which we refer to know ourselves*
	2. Social comparison theory: We compare ourselves with the people in the reference groups to get idea of how we are performing
8. What are the symptoms and causes of groupthink? Be able to provide an example.
	1. *Not realistically evaluate alternative courses of action is due to pressures for unanimity within the group*
	2. Usually due to high group cohesion
	3. For instance, when making a game in a group, after the first design idea comes out, people would not like to iterate on it due to the group think that everyone else is already satisfied with the idea. 
9. Explain the focus of each perspective with regard to groups
	1. Functionalism
		1. *Groups give meaning to individual experience, contributing to social cohesion and stability*
	2. Conflict
		1. *Groups are basis of for the distribution of rewards, privileges, and opportunities*
		2. People in groups can be treated prejudicially. 
	3. Interactionalism
		1. Group norms and goals are generated dynamically during the interactions of the group members.  

## Deviance

1. What is deviance?
	1. Behaviors violating norms
	2. Causing negative reaction
2. Does deviance have a function (Durkheim)?
	1. Clarify *moral boundaries*
	2. *Promotes social cohesion*
	3. Reinforces social norms
3. Describe Structural Strain Theory. Be able to provide examples.
	1. *How people feel about and react to deviance depends on their position in society*
	2. Types of people
		1. Conformist: accept both the norms and goals. 
		2. Innovator: accept goal but doens't agree with norms
		3. Ritualists: accept norms of doesn't reach for goal
		4. Retreatists: Doesn't accept norm and goal, and is trying to be away from them
		5. Rebels: Doesn't accept norm and goal, and is trying to change them
4. Describe Differential Association Theory and Labeling Theory
	1. Differential Association
		1. *we learn to be deviant through our interactions with others who break the rules*
	2. Labeling Theory
		1. Deviance is a result of label. 
5. What is the relationship between the self-fulfilling prophecy and stereotype threat/promise?
	1. Stereotype is a type of self-fulfillign prophecy, which is the fear of performing poorly would cause the person to actually perform poorly. 
6. What are outsiders? How do they view deviance?
	1. Outsiders are people living outside of mainstream society
	2. They accept the deviance and start the labeling process on themselves.
