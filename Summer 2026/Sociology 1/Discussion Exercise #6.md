# Individual and Institutional Discrimination, and Cultural Appropriation

Individual discrimination is the action of a single person. For example, a landlord who denies renting to a black individual is committing individual discrimination. This type of discrimination is usually personal because it occurs between two people and is based on one’s personal beliefs. However, institutional discrimination occurs within social structures in the form of policies that institutionalize discrimination without the conscious intention of individuals. For example, the Federal Housing Administration and private lenders used to deny mortgage insurance to non-white neighborhoods, thus discriminating against blacks. In this situation, there was no need for any federal employee to deny the blacks access; rather, discrimination existed within the institutional setup.

Cultural appropriation is when members of a dominant group adopt elements of a minority group's culture, in aspects including clothing, hairstyles, music, religious symbols, language, but in a way that strips them of their meaning and repackages them as profit. The defining feature is that appropriation flows only from a group that has been marginalized to the group that marginalizes it. Its effects are harmful in several ways. First, the dominant group profits from a culture while the minority that created it stays marginalized and penalized for those same elements. For instance, cornrows and dreadlocks are treated as edgy style when white celebrities wear them, yet Black employees have been fired or told to change their hair for wearing the same hairstyles in the workplace. Second, appropriation flattens a culture into a stereotype, replacing its depth with a caricature. For instance, sacred items could become accessories. Third, the minority group becomes disowned from its own culture, as its traditions are not accepted by dominate culture until those same traditions are repackaged by outsiders.
