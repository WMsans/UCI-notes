# Socialization and Impression Management

Socialization is how we learn the norms, values, and behaviors of our society, and it happens through agents, which is the social groups and institutions that provide structured situations in which socialization takes place. The four main agents are the family, peers, school, and mass media. The family is the single most significant agent because it is the first group we belong to, and most of our primary socialization happens there. For me, my family socialized me by teaching me to respect family norms through clear rewards. When I followed the norms, like showing respect to elders or doing what was asked of me, I received praise or allowance which would turn into arcade game times (old-fashioned childhood entertainment that no longer exists). The link between the behavior and the reward was so clear that I internalized those norms quickly, and I still follow them today without thinking about the reward.

Peers socialize us through interaction with relative equals, and because these relationships are voluntary, their reactions matter a lot. I used to be terrible at memorizing names. When I socialized with more peers, I noticed that starting a conversation by calling by the peer's name made their reaction noticeably warmer, while forgetting their name would do the opposite effects. That feedback from my peers taught me to respect and memorize other people's names, and now I always make an effort to remember them. 

School socialized me by teaching me how to compete with others in all aspects, as it is the first place where we are publicly evaluated on our performance. In some aspect, like extracurricular activities, it feels good as I would usually win in the micro competitions of these environments, while most other comparisons did not feel good at all. Either way, school taught me that comparison and competition are a normal part of life.

Mass media socialized me by exposing me to a large group of people who share my niche hobbies and opinions. Through internet communities I found the indie game community, whose values of artistic integrity and creativity matched my own. Being around so many people who thought like me shaped my identity by pushing me to trust my niche opinions even stronger. 

Impression management, in Goffman's terms, is the effort to control the impressions we make on others so that they form a desired view of us. There are three types of selves we can present: the authentic self, the ideal self, and the tactical self. I show my authentic self when no one is physically around me and no social app is running in the background. In other words, when no socialization is happening at all, which is the only situation that has no stakes and can be honest with myself. 

I show my ideal self during my daily interactions with the people around me, including peers, family, and professors. This is the version of myself I want to be: the responsible student, the good son, the reliable friend. I use the ideal self in everyday situations for its consistency, as presenting the same better version of myself to the people I see regularly builds trust and reputation, and the impression matches the person I am actually trying to become.

I show my tactical self when I want to catch people's attention, such as during club meetings and hiring conventions. In these situations I strategically adapt my presentation to the specific audience. For instance, I emphasize my skills and confidence at a career fair, and my enthusiasm and commitment in a club meeting. In these situations, the goal is to stand out in front of that particular audience, not to be consistent. In addition, I would use the tactical self over the ideal self when the audience and the goal are specific and short-term: a recruiter decides in minutes whether to remember me, and they will never see the consistent daily version of me, so the performance has to be tailored to that exact moment.
