# Scientific Research
See [[Science]]. 
1. Theory must fit the empirical facts
2. Must be testable/falsifiable
3. Can be disproven but never proven

Stages
1. Question
	1. Comes with a theory.
2. Hypothesis derived from the theory
	1. Derive variables from concepts
	2. Independent variable, dependent variable, control variables...
3. Construct a research design (to collect the data)
	1. Internal validity = free of contamination by extraneous influence
	2. External validity: extrent to whihc causal relationships a research settings/population can be generalized outside of the study in the real world
4. Analyze the data
	1. correlations, cross tabulations
	2. Significant does NOT mean important
	3. Significance= the association is greater than might occur at random
5. Draw Conclusions
## Research Designs
### Experimentation: focus on cause/effect
1. Classical
	1. Manipulation of one or more independent variable hypothesized to have a causal impact on the dependent variable
	2. **Randy assignment**
2. Quasi experimental
3. Field experiments
### Field Studies: focus on realism/depth
Observational research entails making systematic observations about behavior as it occurs naturally in an everyday setting.
- Informant Interviewing
- Participant Observation
- Unobtrusive Measures
### Survey Research: focus on realism/breadth
A *survey* is a procedure for collecting information by asking members of some population a set of **questions** and recording their **responses**.
What we ask - *questions/responses*
- Precision, wording, interviewer biases
Who we ask - *sampling*
- Convenience - available
- Random - generalizability, use of statistical theory
	- Clustered
	- Stratified
	- Simple Random
### Archival Analysis: focus on availability
**Archival research** refers to the acquisition and analysis (or re- analysis) of information *already collected by others* (surveys, diaries, media, government records)

**Content Analysis** is a technique for *interpreting* and *coding documents*, such as news paper.

## Research Methods
### Meta analysis
Statistical technique that allows the researcher to **combine the quantitative results** from all previous studies on a question/measurement

Meta-analysis is useful for making sense of **large numbers of studies** on the **same phenomena** that may produce **divergent findings**. 

# Research Ethics
1. Research participants have **rights** that must be respected. 
2. Thus, the research methods are **limited**. 
## Harms
1. Physical harm
	1. Uncommon in sociology research
2. Psychological harm
	1. Manipulations may include providing false negative feedback to participants (ex: participants might be told they did poorly on a test)
	2. Asking very very personal questions
	3. Reduce long term harm by providing a thorough debrief immediately following participation
3. Breach of Confidentiality
	1. Particularly important in surveys and observational research
	2. Precautions vary based on methodology

## Informed consent
1. Potential respondents receive an explanation of the purpose of the research, and a description of the procedures
2. Acknowledgement of any foreseeable risks of participation
3. Description of any benefits to the participants or others
4. List of available medical or psychological resources available in the case of adverse effects
5. Researchers should offer to answer questions about the study when possible
6. Disclosure of the participant’s right to terminate their participation at any time
