# Culture Definition
## Society
Society is a **group of people** who shape their lives in **aggregated** and **patterned** ways that distinguish their group from others
## Culture

**CULTURE** = the system of **symbols** people in a society use to create, store, and organize their affairs
1. Beliefs, 
2. Values, 
3. Norms, 
4. Language, 
5. Patterns of action, 
6. Physical Objects

**SYMBOLS** = object or action that by social convention stands for, represents, something else.
1. Arbitrary but consensual

**LANGUAGE** = made up of **significant** (**shared** with others) symbols
1. A cultural universal
2. **Sapir-Whorf hypothesis**: Does *thought* come before *language* or *language* comes before *thought*?

# Ethnocentrism and cultural relativism
## Ethnocentrism
Using one's culture to evaluate others culture. 
One would label other cultures *wrong* culture.
## Culture relativism
Recognizing that different cultures are merely different, instead of right or wrong, better or worse. 

# Components of Culture
1. Beliefs
	1. Ideas people share about true or false.
2. Values
	1. Ideas that people share about good and bad.
3. Norms
	1. Standards/rules and expectations that people share.
4. Role
	1. Set of normative **requirements**/expectations that apply to the activities of a specific category of people
5. Social Role
	1. the **activities** or expectations of individuals in a specific **social status**
6. Social Status
	1. a recognized position or location within a larger system of social positions
7. Master Status
	1. a status which permeates all other statuses in a status set.
	2. E.g. Age, Gender, Ethnicity, SES
8. Social Groups
	1. relatively small systems of status positions
	2. Primary - small, frequent interaction, intimate relationships
		1. Stronger influence to people
		2. E.g. Family
	3. Secondary - larger, impersonal interaction, segmented relationships
		1. Less important to one, but still defines who they are
		2. E.g. Job
9. Social Institutions
	1. general patterns of social organization or social systems that develop around or focus on accomplishing goals related to basic human and or/social needs
	2. Basic social institutions involve basic needs - Family, Education, Religion, Economics, Polity/Government, Health/Medicine, Mass Media

# Culture Internalized
**INTERNALIZATION** = the process by which we **incorporate** within our personality/self the norms that are prevalent within our society

**CONSCIENCE** = the internal operation of ethical or **moral principles** that control the action, thoughts, and/or feelings of an individual

# Culture Variability
**Dominant culture** = the culture of the group whose values, norms, traditions, and perspectives are imposed on society as a whole
	Dominate is *NOT* *majority*. It is defined by the one in power.

**Subculture** = a subgroup in society whose perspectives and lifestyles *differ* from the dominant culture.

**Counterculture** = a subgroup in society whose beliefs, norms, and values are often **incompatible** with mainstream culture

**Culture Wars** = clashes in mainstream society over values/norms

**Cultural Variability** = cultural differences resulting from Social Change
1. Alterations occur over time
2. Stable and enduring patterns make up society
3. Generation gap

# Cultural Diffusion
**Isomorphism** = when distinct cultures become increasing similar

**Cultural imperialism** = the imposition of one culture’s beliefs and practices on another culture through media and consumer products rather than military force
