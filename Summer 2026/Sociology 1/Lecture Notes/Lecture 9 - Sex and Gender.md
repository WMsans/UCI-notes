# Definition
**Sex** = an individual’s membership in one of two categories- male or female- based on biological factors

**Intersex** = a person whose chromosomes or sex characteristics are neither exclusively male or female

**Gender** = physical, behavioral, and personality traits that a group considers normal for its members
> Masculine and feminine


**Gender identity** = our deeply held sense of self as either male or female
> Gender identity may or may **not correspond** to the sex we were assigned at birth
> 
> When they align, it is **Cisgender**

**Gender expression** = external manifestations of gender
> Clothing, Behavior, Names... 
> So people know how to interact with the expresser

**Gender nonconforming** - gender identity or expression that differs from societal expectations about gender roles

# Essentialist v Constructivist
**Essentialists** see gender as an immutable, biological, unambiguous, two category system
> Bio only. No cultural influences

**Constructivists** see gender as a social construction
> Binary male-female categories are not the only way to classify individuals
> Gender is **symbolic**

# Sexuality 
**Sexual orientation** - the inclination to feel sexual desire toward people of a particular gender or both genders

1. **Heterosexual** =  sexual desire towards the other gender
2. **Homosexuality** = sexual desire towards same gender
3. **Bisexual** = sexual desire towards both genders
4. **Asexual** = lack of sexual attraction of any kind

Kinsey Scale: sexuality falls on a spectrum
Queer Theory: rejects ideas of innate identities or restrictive categories

# Gender role socialization
**Gender role socialization** = the process of learning what constitutes masculinity and femininity
> Perpetuates both **binary** ideas of gender and gender conformity and **heteronormativity**. 

## Agents of socialization
### Family
1. Gender role socialization begins even before birth
2. Learning norms and behaviors through interaction, modelling, rewards
3. Teaching/learning can be intentional or unintentional

### Schools
1. Differences appear in educational experiences based on gender
	1. Statiscially teachers usually favor boys (????)
2. Textbooks can be sexist or stereotypical
3. In academia women still predominate in lower level positions
4. Heteronormativity also pervades school life

### Peers
1. Gender segregation during play
2. Peer groups police gender norms
3. Children gain prestige based on gendered serotypes

### Media
1. Sex role behavior in media is very stereotypical
2. Learn what behavior is appropriate and inappropriate based on gender
3. Sexual diversity not common and often portrayed negatively

# Gender Inequality
Inequality happened in the past: 
1. **Patriarchy** = male domination
2. **Sexism** = belief that one sex (usually male) is superior to another.
> Women experience both institutional and individual discrimination

Inequality based on sexual orientation: 
1. **Homophobia** = prejudice and discrimination toward gay, lesbian, and bisexual people
2. **Transphobia** = prejudice and discrimination toward transgender or other gender nonconforming people
> LGBTQ individuals experience both institutional and individual discrimination

**Microaggressions** = everyday uses of subtle verbal and nonverbal communication that convey denigrating or dismissive messages to members of social groups

## Theories of Gender Inequality
### Functionalism
Argument: Some social roles are better suited to one gender or another
1. Instrumental v Expressive Familial Roles (Parsons 1955)
	1. Instrumental = the family member that provides material support, task oriented, an authority figure and breadwinner
	2. Expressive = the family member that provides emotional support and nurturing
### Functionalism
Argument: Men have historically had access to most of society’s material resources and privileges, and consequently seek to maintain their dominance
1. Gender inequality is a derivative of class inequality (Engels)
	1. Women reproduce the labor force that capitalist rely on for free
	2. Women provide a surplus army of labor when needed
	3. 家庭并非从来就有的，而是历史的产物，其本质由当时的经济制度决定。**现代一夫一妻制**建立在**私有制**和**财产继承**的需要之上。

### Interactionism
Argument: Gender is socially constructed and maintained in everyday life

### Feminism
Applies assumptions about gender inequality to various social institutions

2. Health

	2. Young men more at risk of accidental death

3. Education

	2. **Men** are more likely to **earn more money** per degree granted

	4. Couples in same sex relationships more likely to have advanced degrees but less educated individuals are generally less likely to identify as LGBTQ
	5. 9 out of 10 LGBTQ children report bullying
4. Income
	1. Women’s participation in labor force is increasing over time
	2. Many traditionally female jobs are undervalued and underpaid
	3. Feminization of poverty
	4. Employers can still discriminate against LGBTQ employees in many state
5. Criminal justice
	1. Men more likely to be perpetrators and victims of violent crime
	2. Women more likely to be victims of theft and rape and intimate partner violence
	3. Men outnumber women in every type of arrest except prostitution
	4. More individuals are assaulted because of sexuality than any other social category with the exception race
