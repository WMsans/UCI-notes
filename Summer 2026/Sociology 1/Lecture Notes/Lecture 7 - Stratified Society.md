**Social stratification**: the division of society into groups arranged in a hierarchy
E.g. by age, gender, race, class...

# Inequality

**Social inequality**: the unequal distribution of wealth (*expecially, wealth*), power, or prestige among members of society 

## Principles of inequality
1. Inequality is **characteristic of society** not a reflection of individual differences
2. Social stratification **persists over generations**
3. All societies have stratification systems but the **criteria vary**
	1. For a long period in ancient times, your family. 
4. Stratification is maintained by **beliefs**

# Systems of stratification
1. Slavery
	1. Based on legal ownership
2. Caste
	1. Based on heredity
3. Social class
	1. Based on wealth, property, power, and prestige

## Social Class Stratification
1. Very common since industrial societies
2. So economic stratification is not directly based on race, ethnicity, gender, and age but here are huge amounts of overlap between these variables
3. Intersectionality-identifies how different categories of inequality intersect to shape the lives of individuals and groups

### Conflict Theories of Social Class
1. Marx
	1. Conflict between social groups is central to the workings of society and serves as agent of social change
	2. Capitalism creates two classes- **owners of the means of production** (bourgeoisie) and **workers** (proletariat)
2. Weber
	1. Owning the means of production is not the only way to be successful.
	2. **Wealth** also leads to success
	3. **Prestige** is social honor given to people because of their membership in certain groups
### Structural Functional Theories of Social Class
Stratification is not egalitarian (平等的) but it serves a purpose: 

*We need a variety of roles to be performed and we need incentives for those roles*

Americans largely agree with functionalist arguments. 
However, it's often **unclear which roles are more critical or desirable**, incentives do not always correspond with importance or difficulty

### Symbolic Interaction Theories of Social Class
**Everyday class consciousness**: awareness of one’s own social status and that of others

While our identities may be **structured by preexisting categories**, NOT ourself, we still must **perform** those identities in everyday interactions
