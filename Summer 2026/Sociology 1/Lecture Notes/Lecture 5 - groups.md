# Groups
## Definition
A social unit consisting of 2 or more persons
1. Persons are **recognized as part** of the group
2. There is **interaction** among group members
3. **Goals** are shared by members, interdependent
4. Shared **norms**, common set of expectations

**Social Group** = two or more individuals collectively interacting with their environment with a common identification.

## Types of groups
### Primary Groups
People who are most important to our sense of self;
1. face to face interactions
2. Cooperation
3. Intense feelings of beloning

### Secondary groups
Relative larger, less intimate and personal. 
1. Low identification
2. Often temporary, focused on specific goals

# In-group/out-group
**In-Groups** = Social group we belong to = **We**
**Out-Groups** = Social group we do not belong to = **They**

1. One is either and insider or an outsider.
2. Feelings toward the group are either solidarity for insiders or avoidance, rejection for outsiders
3. Friendliness and cooperation within or unfriendliness and conflict without.
4. Insiders have a feeling of Ethnocentrism about the group (superiority) while outsiders apply negative stereotypes. 

## Intergroup Conflict
**Organized conflict** between organized groups. 

> Instance: nations in war

Research in the minimal group paradigm consistently shows that even **arbitrary group** distinctions can trigger in-group and out- group processes like **ethnocentrism** and biased evaluations of group performance

Out group pressure increases in group bonds. 

Contact with members from the out-group can break down stereotypes. 

## Institutionally Supported contact
Intergroup contact is more likely to reduce stereotyping if it is **backed by social noms that promote equality**. 

## Wintin-group process
Conflict changes structures of participating groups: 
1. More **cohesive**
2. **Leadership** becomes more militant
3. **Winning** the conflict becomes the priority
Norms change; conformity increases

It provides a standard of comparison against which we can evaluate ourselves
1. If we don't see ourselves as having the same desireable qualities, we may adopt a negative self image. 
2. **Social Comparison Theory** = people prefer to compare themselves with other who are similar the salient respects.

# Group Cohesion
**Group cohesion** = Desire to remain in a group
## Social cohesion
because they like one another and want to interact
> Tends to occur when members are similar

## Task cohesion
because they are involved with the group’s tasks
> Will be greater if the goal of the task is valuable, interesting, challenging, and clearly defined

## Source of Cohesion
1. Positive emotion
2. Interdependence

## Consequences of Cohesion
1. Better communication between members
2. Higher quality interactions
3. Members more likely to influence one another
4. Higher conformity
5. Task cohesion can increase productivity and performance

# Group Think
**Group Think** = Not realistically evaluate alternative courses of action is due to pressures for unanimity within the group

## Causes
1. homogeneity of members
2. insulation of the group from its environment
3. lack of clear-cut rules to guide decision making behavior with the group
4. high levels of group stress

## Symptoms 
1. Illusions of invulnerability
2. Illusions of (the group’s) morality
3. Collective rationalization
4. Stereotyping of the adversary
5. Self-censorship
6. Pressure on dissenters
7. Mindguarding
8. Apparent unanimity

## Avoiding Groupthink
1. Group’s **leader** should encourage **dissent**.
2. Leader should be impartial and **not announce a preference** for any particular option or plan.
3. Group should divide itself into several **independent subgroups**.
4. After a tentative consensus has been reached, a group should hold a “**second chance**” meeting.
5. Group can appoint a “**devil’s advocate**” to challenge the position of other group members.

## Authority
**Authority** = the capacity of one member to issue orders

People’s roles/positions within an organization give them certain rights/obligations, and these typically give one person authority over another. 

## Obedience to Authority
**Obedience to Authority** = behavioral conformity through [[#normative influence]] when the Target’s behavior or public attitudes conforms to the Source’s demands on the basis of status or legitimate authority

## Factors of Obedience to Authority
1. Display of status symbols
2. Surveillance
3. Relative Status
4. Group Pressure

# Perspectives on Groups
## Structural Functionalism
Groups help regulate and **give meaning to individual experience**, contributing to social cohesion and stability

## Conflict
Group membership is often the basis of for the **distribution of rewards, privileges, and opportunities**. And individual may be treated **preferentially** or **prejudicially** based on group membership

## Symbolic interactionism
Group norms, values, and dynamics are generated situationally in **interaction with other members**.
