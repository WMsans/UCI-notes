A student staff from the UCI Study Abroad Center came to talk to us about studying abroad. She introduced the different types of programs available. UCEAP lets you study abroad with other UC students and you can still use your financial aid package. There are also partner exchange programs where you pay UCI tuition and fees and receive transfer credit, UCI faculty-led programs where you do an internship or go on excursions with a professor, UC campus-specific programs, and external programs that you apply to directly through the university or a third-party provider. She also mentioned that studying abroad is available all year round, and that one semester abroad gives you the same academic credit as two quarters here. On top of that, it helps you get out of your comfort zone and looks really good on resumes.

A big part of the talk was answering practical questions about finance and life abroad. I was surprised to hear that many programs actually cost less than UCI, and that there are many scholarships available to help cover the rest. She went over the housing options too, including dorms, apartments, and homestays where you stay with a local resident, along with the things you can do while you're in another country, like interning, volunteering, traveling, joining clubs, going to the gym, or playing on a sports team. She also mentioned that every program has a risk management assessment behind it, which is something I hadn't thought about before.

As someone who moved from China to the US a few years ago, the part about getting out of your comfort zone and studying abroad really rang true to me as I know from experience how much you grow from living in a new country. But the talk also made me realize how much planning actually goes into it. One is supposed to start exploring programs about 12 months before departure, apply 10 to 12 months ahead, only apply to one program per term, and make sure you have out-of-pocket money saved up. That whole timeline reminded me of the logistics my family had to figure out when we moved to the US. 

Personally, I don't plan to study abroad, but I still found this session really useful. Now I know that the resource exists and how it works. If I ever change my mind, I know to start with the UCI abroad portal and the UCEAP website, and I'll pass what I learned along to friends who are considering it. In conclusion, it was a very informative session.
