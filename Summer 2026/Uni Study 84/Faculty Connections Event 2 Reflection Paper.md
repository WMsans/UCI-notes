Dr. Michael Dennin shared his framework for succeeding in college. He view college life in three aspects: time, failure, and people, and he added three qualities that students should build: curiosity, confidence, and humbleness. In the speech, he primarily talks about curiosity. 

The parts I remember most clearly are how he connected curiosity and failure and analysis them as tool to learn. Curiosity, he said, means always able to think one more step about why, whether you are doing an assignment or doing something for a professor. On failure, he told us that making mistakes is critical to learning, because mistakes are a sign that you are pushing yourself. He warned us that there is no more high school: in high school, GPA is everything and no mistake is worth making, but in college, taking risks and exploring is the priority. He also gave practical advice on people, including go to clubs, and go see faculty during office hours, but asking about a test is an annoying question, while asking about an academic concept is a great one. When someone asked how to get back up from failures, he simply said to get used to it, and that some failures you can learn from while others you can only regret. Finally, he suggested tinkering around by doing practical projects with your knowledge, such as training mini models if you are in computer science.

This talk really changed how I view my first year. Like Dr. Dennin described, I came into UCI with a high school mindset where grades were everything, and I was afraid the academic rigor here would make me fall behind. His point that mistakes mean you are pushing yourself helped reducing my worries a lot. In addition, his idea about time management is also extremely helpful, stretching myself too thin between lectures, problem sets, and clubs used to be a huge part of my wondering towards college life. After the session, I plan to visit office hours with concept questions instead of test questions, tinker with mini projects to apply what I learn, and join the game development club at Bren Hall that I have been excited about since high school. In conclusion, this talk turned my fear of failing into a plan for growing.
