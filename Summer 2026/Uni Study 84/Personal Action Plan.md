## Goal 1: Join Undergraduate Research in Machine Learning or Computer Graphics

My first goal is to join an undergraduate research project involving machine learning or computer graphics during my first year. I want to learn how research works, apply concepts from my courses, and gain experience solving technical problems that may not have obvious answers. This experience would also help me determine which areas of computer science I would like to pursue in greater depth.

The resources for this goal on campus include: 
- UCI Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP): I will use UROP to learn how to find faculty mentors, prepare for research, and apply for research opportunities or funding.
- Donald Bren School of Information and Computer Sciences faculty and research webpages: I will identify professors and laboratories whose work relates to machine learning, artificial intelligence, computer vision, visualization, or computer graphics.
- Faculty office hours and research talks: These will help me learn about professors’ work and ask informed questions about possible undergraduate opportunities.
- UCI Libraries and online academic databases: I will use these resources to read papers and become familiar with current research topics. ICS also provides an Machine Learning Repository for all students.  
- Technical learning resources: I will strengthen my Python, C++, linear algebra, and relevant machine learning or graphics skills through tutorials and personal projects.

Regarding individuals who might help me, professors whose research interests match mine could explain their current projects and possibly serve as research mentors. Graduate students and current undergraduate researchers could provide me information about what skills their laboratories need and how they became involved. Academic advisors could help me select courses that build the necessary background. Teaching assistants, classmates, and students in technical organizations could also give me feedback on my resume, projects, and emails to faculty members.

For other opportunities to involve, my primary plans include to attend faculty presentations, research workshops, UROP events, and technical club meetings. I would also like to complete a small personal project related to my interests, such as training a simple transformer model or replicate a paper result. A project will give me practical experience and something specific to discuss with potential mentors. Participating in hackathons, project teams, or reading groups could also help me practice collaboration and meet students with similar interests.

One challenge is that I may not initially have enough technical knowledge or research experience. Research positions can also be competitive, and some professors may not have space in their laboratories. I currently feel very ervous about contacting faculty due to the very likely possibility that I got rejected. In addition, balancing research preparation with classes and other responsibilities may be difficult. I will address these barriers by starting with manageable projects, improving my skills consistently, and contacting multiple suitable professors with personalized messages.

The proposed timeline for this task is: 
- Beginning of fall quarter: Explore UROP and ICS research webpages, identify my main areas of interest, and begin a small technical project.
- Middle of fall quarter: Attend research events and office hours, speak with experienced students, and create a résumé that highlights relevant coursework and projects.
- End of fall quarter: Make a list of faculty members whose work interests me, read about their recent research, and begin sending thoughtful, personalized emails.
- Winter and spring quarters: Continue contacting potential mentors, improve my technical preparation, and apply for available research opportunities.
- By the end of my first year: Join a research group if possible or have a clear preparation plan and faculty connections that position me to begin research soon afterward.

To stay motivated, I will need a consistent weekly schedule, clear short-term tasks, and a way to track my progress. I will also recognize smaller achievements such as reading a paper instead of aiming for a position in the beginning. Support from friends, mentors, and classmates will help me remain accountable. Most importantly, I will remind myself that building research skills takes time and that every project and conversation will move me closer to my goal.
## Goal 2: Join and Actively Participate in a Game Development Club

My second goal is to join game development club and become an active member during my first year. I want to meet people who share my interest in games, learn how different parts of game development work together, and contribute to a completed game. This goal also connects with my interest in computer graphics and will allow me to develop both technical and teamwork skills.

The resources for this goal on campus include: 
- UCI CampusGroups and the Anteater Involvement Fair: I will use these resources to find the game development organization, learn about its meetings, and contact its officers.
- Club workshops and project resources: I will attend sessions involving game engines, programming, art, design, audio, or project management.
- Game development tools: Depending on the club’s projects, I will practice with tools such as Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot, Git, and collaborative communication platforms.
- UCI computer labs and maker spaces: These spaces may provide places to work, collaborate, and access useful technology.
- Online documentation and tutorials: I will use official documentation and beginner tutorials when I need to learn a tool outside club meetings.

Regarding individuals who might help me, club officers and project leads in VGDC can help me understand how to join a team and where my skills would be most useful. Experienced club members can provide technical guidance and feedback on my work. Other new members can become teammates as we learn together. Professors, teaching assistants, and students with experience in graphics, programming, art, or music may also offer advice that improves my contributions.

For other opportunities to involve, my primary plans include to attend general meetings, workshops, game jams, social events, and team project sessions. A game jam would be especially helpful since it allows me to experience the full development process within a limited period. I would also like to join a small team as a programmer while open to learning level design, ui design, playtesting, or other roles that the team needs. Contributing to a game from planning through testing and release would help me build communication, time-management, and technical skills.

One challenge may be that I lack enough game development experience to contribute. In addition, club meetings and project deadlines could conflict with coursework or research preparation. Team projects may also face communication problems. To manage these challenges, I would evaluate my current abilities to aim for achievable tasks. I would also force myself to be open to communications. Finally, I would try me best to protect time for academics by limiting my commitments and improve efficiency. 

The proposed timeline for this task is: 
- First two weeks of fall quarter: Find the club at the involvement fair or through CampusGroups, join its communication channels, and attend an introductory meeting.
- First month: Attend at least one workshop or social event, introduce myself to club officers and members, and learn which projects are accepting participants.
- Remainder of fall quarter: Join a project team or game jam and make a small but meaningful contribution.
- Winter and spring quarters: Participate consistently, expand my skills, and take responsibility for larger tasks as I gain experience.
- By the end of my first year: Help complete at least one playable game or prototype and build strong relationships with other club members.

To stay motivated, I would choose projects that genuinely interest me and by working with teammates who communicate and encourage one another. Breaking large tasks into small weekly goals will make progress feel manageable. Regular feedback, visible improvements in the game, and opportunities to playtest our work will help me see the value of my effort. I will also keep a record of what I learn and contribute so that I can recognize my growth throughout the year.
