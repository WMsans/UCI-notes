A librarian walked us through everything UCI's library system has to offer. She started with the basics, like using a VPN when off campus, borrowing with your school ID, and that you can always ask a librarian for help. Then she went through the long list of resources available: study rooms that you can book two hours at a time up to a week in advance (though they get popular around midterms), free newspaper subscriptions like the NYT and WSJ, streaming for movies and documentaries, the Libby app for reading for fun, high-tech equipment you can check out including laptops, iPads, power banks, and even telescopes, Meta VR headsets, and game consoles, and over 900 scholar databases for research. She also mentioned that textbooks can be found through UC Library Search, and that UCI gives us a free download of Microsoft 365.

The point that stood out to me the most was her saying to never pay for anything, because it will always be available through the library. This applies to articles, databases, textbooks, software like Microsoft 365, and even entertainment like streaming and newspapers. Came to the US a few years ago, I've always been very conscious of costs, and I assumed that things like Microsoft 365 or academic articles would always cost money. I never realized the library is behind all of them and that my tuition has already paid for access. It completely changed the way I see the library from merely a quiet place to study to a huge collection of resources that I'm already paying for and should be using.

After the session, I plan to set up Libby so I can read for fun between problem sets and browse the high-tech equipment to see if I can check out a power bank or iPad during long days on campus. When I start doing research, I'll go straight to the scholar databases first instead of paying for articles. I also want to book a study room with friends before midterms or just scramble for a first-come-first-served one. In conclusion, this session made me realize how many resources are hiding in plain sight, and that I should never pay for what the library already gives me.
