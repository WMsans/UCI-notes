# Answer

Entering my first match of *Valorant*, I was ready for being bullied. I thought that competitiveness in the online world depends on hierarchy, where veterans harass newbies just because humiliating someone and putting them below is the easiest way to rise above them. This fear gets validation from the book, as subcultural language includes “the experienced and the newcomer” and, therefore, can be used “as a way to assert one’s position” (Egenfeldt-Nielsen et al., 2020, p. 186). In an hour of playing, I barely got any of this. The chats were limited to “gg” after every match and “W” whenever a teammate played brilliantly getting 5 kills. 

This distinction is the entire conclusion. Grief play, according to the textbook, is defined as the act of intentionally bothering another gamer and is "usually... unrelated to the conditions of winning the game" (p. 185). Bulling a new recruit is precisely what this is: unrelated to the task at hand. *Valorant* (Riot Games, 2020) has made any off-task gameplay costly with merely five members and thirteen rounds to win. As a result, the frustration I had anticipated to turn into bullying was vented onto whoever quit contributing (especially, AFKing). As confirmed by Maher (2016), data published by Riot themselves show almost identical trends: 1% of players being consistently toxic contribute 5% of the total toxicity, while the majority of toxicity comes from regular players on a bad day.

That’s where my confidence in the legitimacy of my evidence begins to fade. I was on a new account and, therefore, probably started off at the shallow end, but years of experience playing other shooter games made me come into it already being capable of adding something to the effort. If there was ever an appearance of acceptance, it could well just be a threshold that I cleared. The cursing that I heard was all targeted at people who lost the team money, and for my one -hour game experience I've not lost the team money yet, which makes their welcoming attitude a "transactional feel" a possibility.
