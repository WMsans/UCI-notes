# Answer

The VGDC game lab clears most of Oldenburg's (1989) eight criteria without argument. The talk, not the machines, is what makes it a third place, and it is also what makes leveling the one criterion I could not call cleanly.

| Criterion                       | Observed  |
| ------------------------------- | --------- |
| Neutral ground                  | Met       |
| Leveling                        | Ambiguous |
| Conversation as main activity   | Ambiguous |
| Accessibility and accommodation | Met       |
| The regulars                    | Met       |
| A low profile                   | Met       |
| Playful mood                    | Met       |
| A home away from home           | Met       |

First criteria is that conversation as main activity. Although the layout of the lab fails criteria since every seat faces a screen (a lab monitor or someone's laptop), what happened fulfilled it the strongest. The play provides the pretext while conversation forms the purpose. For instance, RPG games are always an elephant in the room. There are infinite conversation starters around the room, like is the neutral path in *Undertale* a cowardly act, is the happy ending in *Omori* deserved or just nice? Steinkuehler and Williams (2006) make the exact same claim about the online games: The game provides a motive to meet up while sociability does the work of the third place. Jenkins (2005) provides the explanation behind how this is possible: This is because the room is an evocative place with embedded narratives; the poster boards at the jam do what the town in *Undertale* does, where the story is told without any mechanical motivation.

Second criteria is leveling. This seemed like met but turned out to be conditional. A freshman and a club officer debate as equals regarding the narrative design of the game's ending being good or not here, which would never happen in a class. However, in the lab, there arises a hierarchy of its own. Status is based on canon knowledge: you have beaten *Omori*, completed the genocide route, played it prior to it becoming a meme. On my first visit, most of the time, I was just listening. And it is leveling in the sense of Oldenburg; it does not lack status but status comes from just attending and being interested. In an environment where cast characters and their endings matter (Egenfeldt-Nielsen et al., 2020, p. 211), what matters is knowledge of fiction which you have committed to.
