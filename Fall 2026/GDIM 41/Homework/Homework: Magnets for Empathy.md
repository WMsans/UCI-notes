# Answer

Where the empathy from Undertale actually affected my gameplay is the date with Papyrus. This was due to dramatic irony, or more specifically asymmetric knowledge, after I initially acted cruelly. It is optional, easily skipped, and is initiated by going to his house in snowdin, going up to his bedroom, and talking to him there. It is not required by any route, which is exactly why I played this very casually in the beginning. Since he had just beaten me in the boss fight happened just now for hours, and thus when prompted with answers like "I love it" or "I hate it" for his new outfit or "Open it" or "Don't" for the gift under his hat, I chose the option which would hurt him and thought that he would shrink away. He did not. Saying "I HATE IT" increased his DATE POWER just as much as saying something loving, he took everything I said as flirting, and ended the date thinking that I was "completely obsessed" with him and thus unable to accept my love (Fox, 2015). This is knowledge asymmetry at its purest level, as I understood my meaning but he didn’t, and there was no way for me to clarify his mistake. Papyrus is also a case of a cast character as defined by Egenfeldt-Nielsen et al. (2024): a gatekeeper whose goal emerges from an event where the player doesn’t lose anything. On one hand, this asymmetry allows me to gain empathy for Papyrus due to his kindness and friendliness shown. At some point, I stopped picking cruel options when he offers me help against Undyne and Mettaton, since he had become someone whose goodwill I did not want to squander. But on the other hand, this asymmetry made the process less about empathy. He leaves this date without accepting my love, which I have never asked for. It happened due to the fact that I knew more than him. The theory of that "Bruner's narrative approach views narrative as a way of thinking through which people try to make sense of experience" (1986) is closer to what I felt rather than merely liking as the game narrative may sounds.
